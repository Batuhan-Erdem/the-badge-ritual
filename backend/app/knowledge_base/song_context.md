# Song Context — Knockin' on Heaven's Door

"Knockin' on Heaven's Door" was written by Bob Dylan for the 1973 film "Pat Garrett & Billy the Kid."

The song appears in the context of death, exhaustion, surrender, and the inability to continue carrying a role. Its famous line, "Mama, take this badge off of me / I can't use it anymore," is central to this project.

In the filmic context, the badge can be read as a symbol of duty, authority, identity, and burden. It is not only an object. It is a role the character can no longer carry.

For The Badge Ritual, the badge is transformed from a sheriff's badge into a personal symbolic burden. The user is invited to name a fear, role, memory, duty, guilt, or identity they no longer want to wear.

The song's emotional world is not triumphant. It is tired, quiet, and close to surrender. It does not shout. It lowers its voice. It stands at a threshold.

Important symbolic ideas:
- badge = burden, role, duty, identity, responsibility
- door = threshold, transition, farewell, unknown passage
- knocking = seeking, surrendering, asking permission to cross
- taking the badge off = releasing what can no longer be carried
