# The Badge Ritual: Artist’s Manifesto

## 1. Why This Door Exists

*The Badge Ritual* began with a simple image from Bob Dylan’s *Knockin’ on Heaven’s Door*: a badge that has become too heavy to carry. In the original song, the speaker asks his mother to take the badge away from him because he cannot use it anymore. This is a very small line, but it carries a large emotional weight. The badge is not only a physical object. It is a role, a duty, a burden, and an identity. It represents something a person once needed, but can no longer continue to carry.

This project takes that image and turns it into an interactive digital ritual. The user is asked to write about a “badge” they have been carrying in their own life. This badge may be strength, pride, silence, anger, perfectionism, control, guilt, fear, or any identity that once protected them but later became a burden. The system then transforms that personal input into a symbolic door, a spoken narration, and an interactive release moment.

I chose the form of an interactive web artwork because the subject of the project is not only something to be explained; it is something to be experienced. A static image or a written essay could describe the idea of leaving a burden behind, but interaction allows the user to perform that symbolic action. In this project, the user does not only read about a badge. They write it, see it, hear it, release it, and then knock on a door.

The door is important because it represents a threshold. It is not a simple solution and it does not promise healing. It does not tell the user what to do. It stands between what has been carried and what may come next. The act of knocking is uncertain. The user does not fully know what is behind the door. That uncertainty is part of the artwork. In life, many transitions happen before we know what the next stage will look like. Sometimes a person has to leave something at the threshold before they are ready to continue.

For that reason, *The Badge Ritual* is not designed as a therapy application, a motivational tool, or a normal chatbot. It is a symbolic artwork. It creates a moment where the user can face a burden and transform it into ritual, image, sound, and movement. The main question of the project is not “How can AI solve this problem?” The main question is: “What happens when AI becomes a mirror for something the user has carried silently?”

## 2. Dylan, 1973, and the Weight of the Badge

Bob Dylan wrote *Knockin’ on Heaven’s Door* for Sam Peckinpah’s 1973 Western film *Pat Garrett & Billy the Kid*. In the film, the song appears during a scene of death and farewell. A dying sheriff is losing his strength and approaching the end of his life. The lyric “Mama, take this badge off of me” gives the scene its emotional center. The badge represents law, authority, masculine duty, violence, and the role the character has been forced to live inside.

The cultural atmosphere of 1973 is also important. The United States was shaped by the Vietnam War, anti-war movements, distrust of authority, and a wider questioning of old heroic myths. In that world, the cowboy, the soldier, the sheriff, and the gunman were no longer simple heroes. They were figures carrying the weight of violence, responsibility, and history. Dylan’s song is powerful because it turns that weight into something very human and direct. The speaker is tired. The badge is too heavy. The gun is no longer useful. The door is near.

I wanted the project to carry this historical atmosphere without turning it into a history lecture. The user does not need to read a long explanation before experiencing the artwork. Instead, the historical context is built into the symbols: the badge, the door, the knock, the threshold, the worn materials, the Western-like atmosphere, and the feeling of farewell. The project uses Dylan’s world as an emotional language.

The badge is especially important because it connects the historical world of the song with the personal world of the user. In the song, the badge may belong to a sheriff. In the project, the badge belongs to the user. It may be the badge of always being strong, always being silent, always being successful, always being acceptable, or always being in control. The form changes, but the emotional structure remains similar. A person carries something because they believe they must. Later, that same thing becomes painful.

The project also takes the idea of “heaven’s door” in a symbolic way. It is not only about literal death. It is about endings, transitions, and the moment when one version of the self can no longer continue. In this sense, the door becomes a personal border. The user stands before it with something old in their hands. Before they knock, they must decide what they are ready to put down.

## 3. From Song to Digital Ritual

The project translates the structure of the song into an interactive ritual. Dylan’s song is short, repetitive, and simple, but that simplicity gives it power. It does not explain everything. It leaves space for the listener. I wanted the artwork to do the same. The application does not overload the user with information. It asks a few direct questions, creates a symbolic response, and allows the user to move through a small ritual sequence.

The first part of the ritual is naming. The user names the badge. This is important because many emotional burdens remain vague until they are named. A person may feel tired, angry, afraid, or distant without knowing what identity they have been carrying. By asking the user what badge they wear, the project turns an inner feeling into a symbolic object.

The second part is origin. The user writes when they first began carrying the badge. This adds depth. The badge is not treated as random. It has a history. It may have begun as protection, adaptation, survival, or a response to pain. This is important because the project does not judge the badge. It understands that many burdens once had a purpose.

The third part is cost. The user writes what the badge has taken from them. This is where the ritual becomes honest. A badge may protect a person, but protection can become a prison. Strength can become isolation. Control can become exhaustion. Silence can become loneliness. Perfection can become fear. The project asks the user to see that cost clearly.

After these three inputs, the AI generates a ritual response. It creates a symbolic title, a historical echo, a release text, a door image prompt, a spoken narration, and several guidance texts for the interactive steps. The result is not a diagnosis or advice. It is a poetic reflection. The project avoids saying that the door cures the user. The door is not a doctor and not a solution machine. It is a threshold.

The visual door, the spoken voice, the badge animation, and the knock action all serve this ritual structure. The user first sees the door. Then they hear the narration. Then they release the badge. Then they knock. Finally, the door opens slightly and gives a final symbolic response. This sequence gives the user a sense of movement from naming to release to uncertainty.

## 4. The Role of AI: Tool, Mirror, and Collaborator

In this project, AI has more than one role. It is a tool because it performs technical tasks: generating text, creating image prompts, producing door artwork, and converting narration into speech. But it is also a mirror because it reflects the user’s own words back in a transformed symbolic form. Finally, it becomes a collaborator because the final artwork emerges from the interaction between human input, AI generation, and original code.

The first AI layer is the language model. It receives the user’s personal input and generates structured ritual content. This is not free random text generation. The model is guided by a detailed prompt and a strict JSON structure. It must produce specific fields such as badge title, release text, historical echo, narration, image prompt, door guidance, and material choices. This structure allows the artwork to remain coherent while still being generative.

The second AI layer is image generation. The system uses the generated prompt to create a symbolic door. The door is not chosen from a fixed gallery. It is created according to the user’s badge, the emotional tone, the selected materials, and the atmosphere. This is important because the door should feel personal. A badge of anger should not create the same door as a badge of silence. A badge of control should not feel the same as a badge of grief.

The third AI layer is text-to-speech. The generated narration is turned into a spoken voice. This changes the experience significantly. Reading a text and hearing a voice are emotionally different. The voice makes the ritual feel more intimate, as if someone is sitting beside the user at the threshold. The narration is not meant to be dramatic or robotic. It is meant to be calm, grounded, and human-like.

The project also includes an original procedural soundscape in the frontend. This is not an AI model, but it is an important coded layer. It uses the selected door material to create an ambient atmosphere. Wood, metal, glass, mirror, and stone can all create different sonic feelings. This supports the visual and spoken parts of the artwork.

For me, the most important point is that AI does not replace the human meaning of the project. AI does not decide what the badge means by itself. The user brings the emotional material. I design the structure, rules, interface, and artistic direction. AI transforms the input, but the human gives the transformation its purpose. In this sense, AI is not the author alone. It is a collaborator inside a human-designed ritual.

## 5. The Badge as a Personal Threshold

The badge is the central object of the project. It is small, but it carries the whole emotional weight of the experience. A badge is usually something visible. It tells other people who we are, what role we have, and what authority or identity we represent. But in this project, the badge is also invisible. It represents the emotional roles people wear inside themselves.

Some people wear the badge of strength. They believe they must never look weak. Some wear the badge of silence because they learned that speaking honestly creates conflict. Some wear the badge of success because they are afraid of being ordinary. Some wear the badge of control because uncertainty feels dangerous. Some wear the badge of anger because anger once protected them from feeling powerless.

The project does not treat these badges as simple mistakes. A badge may have helped the user survive a certain period of life. It may have been useful. It may have given structure, protection, or identity. But the project asks what happens when that same badge becomes too heavy. This is the emotional moment that connects the user to Dylan’s song.

In the interface, the badge appears on the door as a separate visual object. This design decision is important. The badge is not permanently painted into the AI-generated image. It is rendered as an interactive frontend element because the user must be able to release it. If the badge were part of the image, it would remain fixed. But as a separate object, it can fall to the threshold. This makes the symbolic action real inside the interface.

When the user clicks the release button, the badge drops from the door to the threshold. This is a simple animation, but it is the emotional center of the experience. The user sees the burden move from identity to ground. It is no longer worn. It is no longer attached. It still exists, but it has changed position. That movement represents the difference between being controlled by a burden and being able to look at it from outside.

## 6. Technical Form as Artistic Meaning

The technical structure of the project is not separate from its artistic meaning. Each technical component supports the ritual.

The frontend creates the stage. It controls the sequence, the visual layout, the badge animation, the audio player, the ambient soundscape, and the knock interaction. The user experience depends on timing and atmosphere. If the project only displayed generated text, it would feel like a normal AI response. The frontend turns the response into an artwork.

The backend creates the generative pipeline. It receives the user’s input, sends it to the language model, receives structured JSON, strengthens the image prompt, calls the image generation service, generates TTS audio, and returns everything to the frontend. This pipeline allows the artwork to be personalized for each user.

The material system is also important. The AI chooses door material, knocker material, badge material, threshold material, atmosphere tone, and light style. These fields make the output more specific. For example, a heavy iron door feels different from fragile glass. A dusty wooden threshold feels different from cold stone. These material choices help translate emotional states into visual and sonic language.

The sound design also has meaning. The spoken narration gives the ritual a human voice. The ambient soundscape creates emotional space around that voice. The knock sound changes according to the material of the door. This means that interaction has texture. The user does not only click a button; they knock on something that feels heavy, fragile, cold, or worn.

This is why the project needed original code. The assignment asks for generative AI at the heart of the creative process, but it also asks for original technical implementation. In this project, AI generation alone would not be enough. The artwork comes from the way AI outputs are arranged, animated, sounded, and experienced through code.

## 7. What Knocking Means in This Project

The knock is the final action of the ritual. In the song, knocking on heaven’s door suggests the nearness of death, farewell, and surrender. In this project, knocking means approaching a threshold after releasing something. The user does not knock at the beginning. They knock only after naming the badge, hearing the narration, and leaving the badge at the threshold.

This order matters. The project suggests that transition requires preparation. A person cannot always move forward while still holding the same burden in the same way. The badge does not disappear magically, but it is placed down. Only then does the door become available.

The door opens only slightly. This is intentional. The project does not show a complete answer behind the door. It does not reveal paradise, victory, or a final solution. It gives a small opening and a symbolic response. This keeps the artwork honest. Real personal transitions are rarely complete in one moment. A door may open a little before a person understands what comes next.

The knock also brings the user into the artwork physically. They are no longer only reading and watching. They must act. Even though the action is digital, it carries symbolic force. The user chooses to knock. That choice is the final gesture of the ritual.

For me, this is the deepest meaning of the project: knocking is not about knowing what is behind the door. It is about becoming ready to approach it. The user may still be uncertain. The badge may still be visible on the ground. The door may only open a little. But something has shifted. The user has named the burden, released it from their identity, and taken one step toward the unknown.

## 8. Conclusion: Leaving the Badge at the Door

*The Badge Ritual* is a project about burdens, identities, and thresholds. It uses Bob Dylan’s *Knockin’ on Heaven’s Door* not as decoration, but as the emotional foundation of the artwork. The song’s badge becomes the user’s badge. The song’s door becomes a personal threshold. The song’s farewell becomes a digital ritual of release.

The project combines large language model generation, text-to-image generation, text-to-speech generation, and original interactive code. These techniques are not used only to show technical ability. They are used to create an experience where text, image, sound, and interaction work together.

The most important idea is that AI can become a mirror when it is placed inside a meaningful human structure. If the user gives the system a personal burden, AI can transform it into symbols. But the value of that transformation comes from the human context: the song, the history, the interface, the ritual, and the user’s own reflection.

In the end, the project does not claim that a person can be healed by clicking a button. It does not claim that a door can solve a life. Instead, it creates a small symbolic moment. A person writes what they have carried. A door appears. A voice speaks. A badge falls. A hand knocks. The door opens slightly.

That small opening is the meaning of the project. Not a full answer, not a final cure, but a threshold. The badge is still part of the story, but it is no longer attached to the body. It has been placed at the door.

And then, finally, the user knocks.
